About Davikis Dice

Davikis Dice is one person, a workbench, and a lot of resin. Every set is mixed, poured, cured, sanded, and inked by hand, which is why stock counts are small and why many sets are made exactly once.

The shop exists because of a gap. Plenty of makers cast a beautiful standard seven, but almost nobody casts a d3, a d15, or a d24. Those shapes come up constantly in homebrew rules and in systems other than D&D, and players have been going without them.

How A Set Gets Made

  1. Mixing

    Resin, pigment, and any inclusions are measured and stirred slowly to keep bubbles out.

  2. Pouring

    Each die is poured individually into a silicone mold and left under pressure.

  3. Curing

    The set sits for 24 hours before it can be handled at all.

  4. Sanding

    Nine grits, from removing the pour spout down to a polish. This is the slow part.

  5. Inking

    Numbers are filled by hand, wiped back, and left to set.

Care

Keep resin dice out of direct sunlight, which fades pigment over time, and away from hard stone rolling surfaces, which chip sharp edges. A lined tray is worth having.
